Rome and Tuscany are the most-asked-for combination on the Italy desk,
and they reward planning more than almost any other Italian itinerary.
A week split between the city and the cypress hills, designed
correctly, is the trip that most clients tell us afterward they
underestimated. We have built this trip enough times to know exactly
how it should be paced.

## The week we design

Three nights in Rome, four in the country. Rome first — arrive jet-
lagged, recover at the hotel, dinner reserved for the second night
when you can taste it. Two days of the city: a private guide for the
Vatican Museums (early entry, the room with the Raphael frescoes
before the crowds), an afternoon in Trastevere on foot, the proper
plate of cacio e pepe at the restaurant we send clients to (not the
one on the magazine list).

Then a private driver to the Val d'Orcia — about two and a half hours
on the Autostrada — and four nights in a farmhouse villa or at one of
the small country hotels we work with. Days are slow: a private
truffle hunt, a lesson at the cooking school in Pienza, a walk through
the cellars at a Brunello producer, a long lunch at a trattoria where
they don't take reservations from anyone we haven't introduced.

## Who it suits

Couples on a long-overdue trip. Multigenerational families when the
villa is right (Reschio holds eight to fourteen). Solo women — Lisa
plans these almost weekly. First-time-to-Italy travelers who don't
want a packaged tour.

## What we get right

The hotel rooms with the actual Roman view, not the air-shaft view.
The Vatican guide who is also a published art historian. The villa
caretaker in Tuscany who'll cook the first night's dinner and get the
guests over their initial overwhelm. The driver between Rome and the
country who knows the lunch stop in Orvieto.

## When to plan

Eight to twelve months for spring and fall. Six months in summer
shoulder. The villas in the Val d'Orcia book the earliest — a year
ahead is not too soon for August.

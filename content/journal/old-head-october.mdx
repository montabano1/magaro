There's a moment, walking the par-3 sixteenth at Old Head, when the
wind rises and the lighthouse goes very quiet, and you understand
why this golf course was built where it was. Not for ease. For
honesty.

We've sent clients to Old Head in nearly every month it's open. Most
ask for July — long days, family-friendly weather. We push, gently,
toward October.

## Why autumn

The light goes long and gold at four o'clock. The crowds have thinned.
You can stand on the third tee box and not see another foursome in
either direction. Yes, the weather turns. But that's also when the
manor house earns its keep — peat fire, a serious whiskey list, a
chef who'll send out a small plate of langoustines if your round
runs late.

> The trick to Old Head in October is not to fight the weather.
> It's to plan around it.

## The routine

A typical day, on a five-day Old Head trip:

- **8:30 — breakfast at the manor.** Strong coffee, brown bread.
- **10:24 — first tee, with caddies arranged the night before.** Always book
  caddies. The wind will fool you on five holes; they won't.
- **15:00 — late lunch in town.** A walk through Kinsale; an oyster
  at Bastion if it's open.
- **18:00 — second nine, if the light holds.** Otherwise, the bar.

Two days at Old Head. One at Lahinch. One at Ballybunion. A travel
day to Adare Manor for a different kind of evening, and home from
Shannon. That's the trip we send.

## When to plan

Tee times open eight to ten months out. We start booking October
trips the previous January. If you're thinking about it, the time
to talk is now.

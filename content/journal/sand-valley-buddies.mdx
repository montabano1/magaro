A friend asked me, last month: what actually goes into running one
of these things? You make it look easy. It's not, particularly. But
the structure is teachable. So here's the structure.

## The math

Eight golfers. Five days. Sand Valley is the destination. That
gives you three full days of golf at the resort, one travel-in day,
and one travel-out day. Add Erin Hills as a side trip — a
ninety-minute drive — and you have the architecture.

## The mistakes most people make

**Booking too tight.** Five guys want to play 36 a day. Three want
to play 18 and nap. The trick is structuring the tee times so the
36-a-day guys get their fill without the 18-and-nap guys sitting
around feeling guilty.

**Cars.** Every buddies trip needs one more rental car than you
think. Always.

**Caddie pairing.** Pair the low handicap with the high handicap.
The pace works out. The conversations are better.

**The dinner reservation.** Most resorts will take a group of eight
on short notice. Most resorts will *quietly resent* it. Book the
dinners three months out.

## The day we got right

Day three at Sand Valley:

- **6:45.** First foursome, Mammoth Dunes. Walking, caddies.
- **7:00.** Second foursome, fifteen minutes behind. Walking,
  caddies.
- **11:30.** Lunch at the clubhouse. The lobster roll, if it's on.
- **13:30.** Both groups on Sedge Valley. Bigger swings, lower
  expectations.
- **18:00.** Steaks at the lodge. The eight bags of laundry that
  arrived back in the rooms while we played.
- **20:30.** The fire pit. The cigars. The story about the second
  shot on fourteen, which gets better every year.

That's a remarkable day. That's the trip.
